### Quantiles

1. The 13 TeV dilepton (Î±_s-style) fit doesn't use the angles by default. mz_dilepton.py fits --axes mll ptll (plus rapidity variants); the CS angular variables are an option (--csVarsHist), used for angular-coefficient studies and validation â not stuffed into the Î±_s observable for extra precision.
2. Where the 13 TeV analysis does fit angle-sensitive observables, it pays the full price to make that legitimate. The W-mass fit observable (muon pT, Î·, charge) is itself a convolution of ptV, yV and decay angle â same "leakage" mechanism as yours, exploited deliberately. But look at what's in that model, and in mz_dilepton.py right next to the axes: muon efficiency scale factors with hundreds of stat+syst nuisance parameters binned in muon pT/Î·, the muon momentum-scale calibration with its own systematics (muonScaleSyst_responseWeights...), resolution, prefiring â i.e. the detector acceptance "mirror" comes with uncertainties attached, so the fit can only use angular structure to the extent data constrains the detector model. On top of that, the theory side is helicity-decomposed: the angular coefficients (AââAâ) get their own QCD-scale nuisances, so physical angular structure has dedicated freedom instead of being assumed perfect.
3. Your 5 TeV setup has none of that yet â zero efficiency systematics, zero momentum-scale systematics, theory templates with no physical angular content at all. Same trick, but with the detector assumed flawless. That's the entire difference.

So the honest framing for your supervisor isn't "this approach is wrong" â it's: "the 13 TeV analysis earns its angular sensitivity by carrying a complete experimental-systematics model; in my current simplified setup the same construction turns into an unprotected assumption, so I fit ptllâyll until the efficiency and momentum-scale uncertainties are implemented." Which also means the work isn't wasted: the 4D histograms plus the scarekit uncertainties (landing Thursday) and eventually efficiency SFs are exactly the path to legitimately revisiting it â and the factorization switch gives you the A/B test to show how much angular sensitivity you've earned at each step.
Helicity smoothing revisit: if you ever move the fit to the finer angular observables (the 4D cosÎ¸*/Ï* setup from the earlier study) â there per-bin statistics drop sharply and this smoothing becomes genuinely useful, alongside the --factorizeSystAxes option that already exists as a lighter-weight version of the same idea.

### DONE (Aug 6): 5 TeV now fits ptll < 44, matching 13 TeV

Was: 5 TeV had 40 ptll bins (0 ... 37 44 100), 13 TeV 39 (0 ... 37 44). Edges
IDENTICAL through 44; 5 TeV carried one extra wide 44-100 bin holding 5.62% of

--- how to cut it -------------------------------------------------------------
  setupRabbit_5TeV.py --ptllMax 44

Crops the ptll axis when the tensor is built. _crop_ptll() runs inside
_scale_mc() and the un-scaled branch of _get_hist(), so EVERY histogram gets it
(nominal, all theory + experimental variations, and Asimov data, which is built
from the already-cropped MC). A missed call site would fail loudly on a shape
mismatch in tensorwriter, not silently misalign.

Do NOT edit dilepton_ptV_binning: axis_ptVGen (mz_5TeV.py:206) already does
[:-1] and would become [:-2]. Only axis_ptll would need changing, and that
alters the response-matrix construction (response_matrix.py:226 appends the
(44,100] gen overflow edge) -- untested, and it costs a full histmaker rerun.

The response matrix needs NO change: R stays (40,10,40,10) and param_model.py
:605 _crop_R_to_fit crops it, exactly the case its comment describes ("R has
ptll [0, ..., 44, 100] while the fit ends at 44"). Verified: the fit ran clean,
edmval 6.0e-29, no shape error.

EQUIVALENT to the 13 TeV construction, not just similar. They end the axis at
44 with overflow=True and flow=False, so events >44 land in an ignored
overflow; we drop the bin. Same 39 bins, same edges, same events fitted.
Advantage of the crop: the histmaker output KEEPS the 44-100 bin, so the cut is
a one-flag A/B switch instead of a reprocessing.

### Plotting fit templates: rabbit_plot_hists.py (what actually gets fitted)

Luca's preferred way to look at a variation:

  rabbit_plot_hists.py <fitresults.hdf5> --prefit --varNames pdfAlphaS

This plots the DATACARD templates the fit actually sees (after symmetrization,
after the ptll x yll -> ptll projection, after the tensor's bookkeeping).
makeDataMCStackPlot.py instead plots the raw HISTMAKER histograms. They should
agree, but only rabbit_plot_hists is guaranteed to be what is fitted.

The difference matters most for b,c quark masses: the histmaker stores 16 raw
MSHT20 PDF members (mbrange 7, mcrange 9), while the fit only ever sees 4
symmetrized nuisances -- pdfMSHT20{mb,mc}range{SymAvg,SymDiff}. Comparing raw
members is NOT the same as comparing what the fit uses.

--- GOTCHA: the fitresult must contain saved histograms ---------------------
rabbit_plot_hists.py takes a rabbit FITRESULT (not a datacard), and it needs
histograms saved in it. Our production fits do NOT have them, because
--saveHists and friends were deliberately stripped to avoid the hessian-step
OOM. Symptom: nothing to plot.

Luca's reference DOES have them -- verified directly:
  /ceph/.../lavezzo/alphaS/260717_2D_wallmargin_0/cov/fitresults.hdf5
  mappings["Project ch0 ptll"]["channels"]["ch0"] contains
  hist_prefit_inclusive_variations (and the postfit/global_impacts ones).

THE THREE STEPS
---------------

  idip     probe = loose muon + isGlobal  pass = Muon_mediumId

HOW THE PER-MUON TRIGGER IS EXTRACTED (no trigger objects needed)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
TrigObj holds NO muon objects in this production (0 entries with id == 13 in
5.16M events; only id 11, electrons). Muon_triggerIdLoose is an offline quality
flag mirroring the HLT muon ID, NOT a record that the muon fired, and the L1_*
branches are event-level seed decisions. So no muon can be tagged.
But lowPU's own combination formula, read BACKWARDS, extracts eps per muon from
the only observable there is -- whether the EVENT fired, as a function of BOTH
muons' bins:
    P(fires | muons in bins a, b) = 1 - (1 - eps_a)(1 - eps_b)
fitted by maximum likelihood over the (bin1, bin2, pass) counts, with an
analytic gradient and the exact Hessian.
  VALIDATION: toys give bias +9e-5 on flat truth; injected dips of 0.70 and 0.80
  come back 0.7063+-0.0355 and 0.7923+-0.0268 (all pulls < 1 sigma); the implied
  event rate reproduces the input to 2e-6.
  REAL DATA (full sample, 12 eta x 10 pt, charge-merged = 120 bins):
     data  86705 events,     925 double failures,  0/120 at a bound, 120 modes
     MC   3824376 events,    236 double failures, 13/120 at a bound, 107 modes
     eps_data mean 0.894, eps_MC ~0.99
  The information budget is the DOUBLE-failure count: only events where BOTH
  muons fail constrain eps, and that rate goes as (1-eps)^2. Hence 236 in 3.8M
  MC events, and hence 13 MC bins with no double failures at all -- those are
  held at the fitted value with zero uncertainty and carry no nuisance.
  THE NUISANCES ARE EIGENVECTORS of the fit covariance, not one per bin. lowPU
  can use per-bin nuisances because its map comes from a TAGGED TnP where bins
  are nearly independent; here a single event constrains a PRODUCT, so the
  overall (1-eps) scale is near-degenerate with the bin structure (|rho| up to
  0.7; pulls from the marginal diagonal errors come out 0.46-0.84 wide instead
  of 1). Eigenvectors are the representation the 13 TeV SMOOTH SFs already use.

  because their probes already passed reco+tracking -- which is exactly why reco

WHAT IT DOES TO THE FIT
-----------------------
  Applying the eta map to MC and projecting onto the REAL fit axes:
     normalisation (mean event weight) ... 0.9882   = -1.18%
     ptll  template, peak-to-peak shape .. 0.03%
     yll   template, peak-to-peak shape .. 0.52%, confined to the two outermost
            bins; central bins move +0.03 to +0.09%
  So the ID SF is dominated by a 1.18% NORMALISATION, which the 1.9% lumi
  nuisance largely absorbs; alpha_s lives in the ptll shape where the effect is
  0.03%. Expect a SMALL change in sigma(alpha_s), not a large one.

GOTCHAS (each cost real time)
-----------------------------
  * NEVER book a step after its own cut. Measuring the ID after the ID filter
    gives 1 by construction; same for the trigger after HLT_HIMu17. The
    measurement run relaxes the ID to loose and replaces the HLT_HIMu17 filter
    with HLT_HIMu17 || HLT_HIL3DoubleMu0 (--muonEffProbeTrigger).
  * That OR is not optional for speed either: dropping the trigger filter
    entirely pushes all 5.16M events/file through the selection and the job goes
    from ~5 min to >12 h (job 6614264 timed out exactly so).
  * Measure fine, coarsen later. Rebinning cannot un-average structure.
  * Empty map cells (SF 1, variance 0) give variations identical to nominal = a
    FLAT direction in the likelihood, not a small uncertainty. Pruned in
    setupRabbit; the factories warn how many there are.
  * Tensor axes must not share a name with a fit axis -- hist raises "Hist
    instance cannot contain axes with duplicated names". Hence effBinYll /
    effBinPtll rather than yll / ptll.
  * The C++ helpers take (float, float); ptll and yll are DOUBLE in the
    histmaker and cppyy will not narrow them ("could not convert argument 1").
    Define float copies first.
  * mz_5TeV.py --help shows only the FIRST-STAGE parser, so --muonCorr,
    --muonEfficiencyHists, --muonEffEtaBins are all absent from it yet work.
  * ROOT::VecOps::Map with a lambda and RVec<int>(RVec<bool>) do not compile in
    this build; use ROOT::VecOps::Where.
  * The production histmaker is ~10 MIN, not hours (build graphs 77 s, lumi 11 s,
    event loop 454 s for 882M events at -j 16). Queue time is the variable.

*** THE 17% RISE IS REAL, AND IT IS ENTIRELY THE yll DIMENSION. ***
  Ruled out, each by direct test:
   - the nuisances: 8 vs 269 vs 432 all give 1.753-1.755, and ZERO efficiency
     nuisances gives 1.7527. So it is the NOMINAL TEMPLATES, not the nuisances.
   - the normalisation: the SF drops the yield 2.2%, but applying a flat
     --lumiScale 0.97787 to the no-efficiency production gives 1.4970, i.e.
     nothing. stat moves 0.3203 -> 0.3239, exactly the 1.1% expected.
   - the ptll shape: 0.037% peak to peak.
   - lost alpha_s sensitivity: the pdfas template's ptll shape deviation is
     4.232% both with and without the SF -- IDENTICAL.
   - the code version: today's code with NO efficiency flags reproduces the July
     production BIT-IDENTICALLY (1.4933, lumi 0.7236, theory 1.2556). This
     control matters -- the first "baseline" compared a July file against a
     production built today, which was a confound.
  What is left: the yll shape, 0.749% peak to peak.
  MECHANISM: yll does enormous work in this fit -- it takes sigma(alpha_s) from
  3.1271 (1D) to 1.4933 (2D), a factor of two, by constraining the
  normalisation-like nuisances (luminosity impact 0.0005 in 1D, 0.7236 in 2D).
  The SF's 0.749% yll distortion eats into that constraining power and pushes
  sigma back toward the 1D value. In 1D the SF costs NOTHING (3.1271 -> 3.1293).
  Row-by-row: luminosity 0.7236 -> 0.3657, theory 1.2556 -> 1.5121, resumTNP
  0.7408 -> 0.9887, pdfCT18Z 0.6611 -> 0.8087, muon_eff_all 0.0839.
  => ASK LUCA whether a 0.75% yll shape should cost 17%, and whether the 2D yll
     constraint is robust enough to lean on. Do NOT quote 1.7547 before that.

  effSystTnP input, measured (probe-selection envelope):
     reco  eps_data 0.9908 nominal -> 0.9945 alt, SF shift +0.09%
     idip  eps_data 0.9915 nominal -> 0.9923 alt, SF shift -0.04%
     per-cell shift: reco median 0.025% max 0.860%; idip median 0.041% max 2.114%
  CAVEAT: the per-cell maxima are NOISE, not physics. The alternative map carries
  its own ~0.3% per-cell error, so differencing two maps gives per-cell spreads
  of that order from statistics alone, an order of magnitude above the median
  shift. 13 TeV's altSig does not suffer this because it refits the SAME data
  with a different model, so the fluctuation is common and cancels; ours comes
  from a different probe sample. Since effSystTnP is ONE nuisance per step
  anyway, apply it as a FLAT per-step shift (+0.09% / -0.04%), not per cell.

IMPLEMENTATION DIRECTION: ADAPT THE PR #709 IN-SITU EFFICIENCY WORKFLOW
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Decision/direction (Oct 2 2026): replace the external 5 TeV reco/idip SF-map
plus inferred per-muon trigger-fit approach with the in-situ efficiency workflow
shown in WMass/WRemnants PR #709. This is an adaptation to 5 TeV, not a direct
cherry-pick: the PR machinery is built around the 13 TeV W/Z histmakers, its
existing SF maps, selection categories, and fit setup.

WHAT THE PR WORKFLOW DOES (from the PR #709 diff shared in chat)
----------------------------------------------------------------
  * Adds muon_efficiencies_insitu.py and an in-situ helper in
    muon_efficiencies_smooth.hpp. ID, HLT and isolation efficiency corrections
    are parameterized with Chebyshev coefficients in eta slices (ID in pT;
    trigger/iso in pT and recoil uT, with charge treatment by step).
  * The histmaker evaluates linearized MC efficiency-weight variations and
    books them as muonInsituEff templates. Rabbit registers these as
    unconstrained fit nuisances, so the selected data constrain efficiencies
    together with the physics parameters instead of importing an external
    TnP SF as the central correction.
  * make_insitu_effSF.py reads fitted nuisance values and iterates the central
    efficiency correction; make_insitu_effSF_from_tnp.py can instead project
    existing external TnP SFs onto the same basis for a starting point.

5 TeV ADAPTATION TO IMPLEMENT
-----------------------------
  1. Port the in-situ parameterization/helper and MC efficiency inputs into the
     5 TeV dilepton histmaker. First establish the 5 TeV basis variables and
     parameter labels; do not carry over uT or charge dependence by default.
  2. Add pass/fail efficiency categories to the 5 TeV Z selection and book the
     in-situ variation tensor on the same fit axes. The parameterization must
     describe the selected 5 TeV observables without using the current
     external reco/idip maps or trigger-fit eigenmodes as additional corrections.
  3. Wire the 5 TeV Rabbit setup to register and group the in-situ nuisances,
     then implement the fit-result-to-central-SF iteration and convergence
     checks for 5 TeV. Preserve the central value used for each production so
     the card constrains variations around the matching expansion point.
  4. Validate closure on simulation, fit stability/identifiability, nuisance
     correlations, and the effect on ptll/yll templates before treating the
     result as an efficiency calibration.

IMPORTANT 5 TeV DIFFERENCES / OPEN DECISIONS
-------------------------------------------
  * No usable muon TrigObj entries were found in the sampled 5 TeV NanoAOD.
    The in-situ route may avoid requiring a per-muon trigger tag, but its HLT
    category/likelihood still needs an explicit event-level definition and
    validation. Do not silently reuse the pair-fit assumption
    P(fire)=1-(1-e1)(1-e2).
  * Current 5 TeV selection has no isolation requirement, so the PR's Iso leg
    is not automatically part of this analysis. Decide whether it should be
    omitted or whether the 5 TeV selection should change.
  * 5 TeV has two selected muons and fits ptll x yll. Define how both legs enter
    the event weights/categories and whether the in-situ parameters are shared
    across legs and charges.
  * Decide which detector effects are floated in situ (mediumId, isGlobal,
    HLT), and whether existing scarekit momentum corrections and their
    uncertainties remain external. Avoid double-applying existing SFs.
  * The 5 TeV Z sample, event trigger paths, backgrounds, statistics and fit
    observables differ from the PR's 13 TeV use. The analysis team must approve
    the efficiency model, parameterization granularity/regularization, and
    category definitions before implementation is considered physics-valid.

### Quantiles

1. The 13 TeV dilepton (Î±_s-style) fit doesn't use the angles by default. mz_dilepton.py fits --axes mll ptll (plus rapidity variants); the CS angular variables are an option (--csVarsHist), used for angular-coefficient studies and validation â not stuffed into the Î±_s observable for extra precision.
2. Where the 13 TeV analysis does fit angle-sensitive observables, it pays the full price to make that legitimate. The W-mass fit observable (muon pT, Î·, charge) is itself a convolution of ptV, yV and decay angle â same "leakage" mechanism as yours, exploited deliberately. But look at what's in that model, and in mz_dilepton.py right next to the axes: muon efficiency scale factors with hundreds of stat+syst nuisance parameters binned in muon pT/Î·, the muon momentum-scale calibration with its own systematics (muonScaleSyst_responseWeights...), resolution, prefiring â i.e. the detector acceptance "mirror" comes with uncertainties attached, so the fit can only use angular structure to the extent data constrains the detector model. On top of that, the theory side is helicity-decomposed: the angular coefficients (AââAâ) get their own QCD-scale nuisances, so physical angular structure has dedicated freedom instead of being assumed perfect.
3. Your 5 TeV setup has none of that yet â zero efficiency systematics, zero momentum-scale systematics, theory templates with no physical angular content at all. Same trick, but with the detector assumed flawless. That's the entire difference.

So the honest framing for your supervisor isn't "this approach is wrong" â it's: "the 13 TeV analysis earns its angular sensitivity by carrying a complete experimental-systematics model; in my current simplified setup the same construction turns into an unprotected assumption, so I fit ptllâyll until the efficiency and momentum-scale uncertainties are implemented." Which also means the work isn't wasted: the 4D histograms plus the scarekit uncertainties (landing Thursday) and eventually efficiency SFs are exactly the path to legitimately revisiting it â and the factorization switch gives you the A/B test to show how much angular sensitivity you've earned at each step.
Helicity smoothing revisit: if you ever move the fit to the finer angular observables (the 4D cosÎ¸*/Ï* setup from the earlier study) â there per-bin statistics drop sharply and this smoothing becomes genuinely useful, alongside the --factorizeSystAxes option that already exists as a lighter-weight version of the same idea.

### Explanation

- theory_corr_weight_map in theory_corrections.py has entries for our exact
  corrections (scetlib_dyturbo_LatticeNP_CT18Z_N3p0LL_N2LO_pdfvars and _pdfas).
  For corrections in that map, define_theory_corr_weight_column builds a
  per-variation weight tensor that INCLUDES the event's LHE CT18Z weight
  (LHEPdfWeightAltSet11), and mz_5TeV.py already routes it into the helper.
- The correction files divide each SCETlib grid member by the MiNNLO spectrum
  reweighted with the SAME LHE member (verified: minnlo_ref_hist in the 5020GeV
  pickles has a 59-entry vars axis for pdfvars, as0118/16/20 for pdfas).
- Net per-variation weight: sigma_UL response comes purely from the SCETlib
  grids (the LHE sigma_UL part cancels in the ratio), the angular-coefficient
  response comes purely from the LHE weights. That is exactly the 13 TeV
  scheme; their pdf_from_corr switch makes grids and LHE weights strict
  alternatives for sigma_UL, never added twice.
- The 13 TeV "ByHelicity" histograms are a smoothing device (replace per-event
  LHE weights by gen-binned helicity-moment ratios to suppress MC noise). Our
  templates have negligible noise in the 2D binning, so this adds nothing here.

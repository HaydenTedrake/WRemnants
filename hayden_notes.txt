### Quantiles

1. The 13 TeV dilepton (Î±_s-style) fit doesn't use the angles by default. mz_dilepton.py fits --axes mll ptll (plus rapidity variants); the CS angular variables are an option (--csVarsHist), used for angular-coefficient studies and validation â not stuffed into the Î±_s observable for extra precision.
2. Where the 13 TeV analysis does fit angle-sensitive observables, it pays the full price to make that legitimate. The W-mass fit observable (muon pT, Î·, charge) is itself a convolution of ptV, yV and decay angle â same "leakage" mechanism as yours, exploited deliberately. But look at what's in that model, and in mz_dilepton.py right next to the axes: muon efficiency scale factors with hundreds of stat+syst nuisance parameters binned in muon pT/Î·, the muon momentum-scale calibration with its own systematics (muonScaleSyst_responseWeights...), resolution, prefiring â i.e. the detector acceptance "mirror" comes with uncertainties attached, so the fit can only use angular structure to the extent data constrains the detector model. On top of that, the theory side is helicity-decomposed: the angular coefficients (AââAâ) get their own QCD-scale nuisances, so physical angular structure has dedicated freedom instead of being assumed perfect.
3. Your 5 TeV setup has none of that yet â zero efficiency systematics, zero momentum-scale systematics, theory templates with no physical angular content at all. Same trick, but with the detector assumed flawless. That's the entire difference.

So the honest framing for your supervisor isn't "this approach is wrong" â it's: "the 13 TeV analysis earns its angular sensitivity by carrying a complete experimental-systematics model; in my current simplified setup the same construction turns into an unprotected assumption, so I fit ptllâyll until the efficiency and momentum-scale uncertainties are implemented." Which also means the work isn't wasted: the 4D histograms plus the scarekit uncertainties (landing Thursday) and eventually efficiency SFs are exactly the path to legitimately revisiting it â and the factorization switch gives you the A/B test to show how much angular sensitivity you've earned at each step.
Helicity smoothing revisit: if you ever move the fit to the finer angular observables (the 4D cosÎ¸*/Ï* setup from the earlier study) â there per-bin statistics drop sharply and this smoothing becomes genuinely useful, alongside the --factorizeSystAxes option that already exists as a lighter-weight version of the same idea.

### DONE (Aug 6): 5 TeV now fits ptll < 44, matching 13 TeV

Was: 5 TeV had 40 ptll bins (0 ... 37 44 100), 13 TeV 39 (0 ... 37 44). Edges
IDENTICAL through 44; 5 TeV carried one extra wide 44-100 bin holding 5.62% of
events, so the 5-vs-13 impact comparison was NOT over the same phase space.

--- how to cut it -------------------------------------------------------------
  setupRabbit_5TeV.py --ptllMax 44

Crops the ptll axis when the tensor is built. _crop_ptll() runs inside
_scale_mc() and the un-scaled branch of _get_hist(), so EVERY histogram gets it
(nominal, all theory + experimental variations, and Asimov data, which is built
from the already-cropped MC). A missed call site would fail loudly on a shape
mismatch in tensorwriter, not silently misalign.

Do NOT edit dilepton_ptV_binning: axis_ptVGen (mz_5TeV.py:206) already does
[:-1] and would become [:-2]. Only axis_ptll would need changing, and that
alters the response-matrix construction (response_matrix.py:226 appends the
(44,100] gen overflow edge) -- untested, and it costs a full histmaker rerun.

The response matrix needs NO change: R stays (40,10,40,10) and param_model.py
:605 _crop_R_to_fit crops it, exactly the case its comment describes ("R has
ptll [0, ..., 44, 100] while the fit ends at 44"). Verified: the fit ran clean,
edmval 6.0e-29, no shape error.

EQUIVALENT to the 13 TeV construction, not just similar. They end the axis at
44 with overflow=True and flow=False, so events >44 land in an ignored
overflow; we drop the bin. Same 39 bins, same edges, same events fitted.
Advantage of the crop: the histmaker output KEEPS the 44-100 bin, so the cut is
a one-flag A/B switch instead of a reprocessing.

### Plotting fit templates: rabbit_plot_hists.py (what actually gets fitted)

Luca's preferred way to look at a variation:

  rabbit_plot_hists.py <fitresults.hdf5> --prefit --varNames pdfAlphaS

This plots the DATACARD templates the fit actually sees (after symmetrization,
after the ptll x yll -> ptll projection, after the tensor's bookkeeping).
makeDataMCStackPlot.py instead plots the raw HISTMAKER histograms. They should
agree, but only rabbit_plot_hists is guaranteed to be what is fitted.

The difference matters most for b,c quark masses: the histmaker stores 16 raw
MSHT20 PDF members (mbrange 7, mcrange 9), while the fit only ever sees 4
symmetrized nuisances -- pdfMSHT20{mb,mc}range{SymAvg,SymDiff}. Comparing raw
members is NOT the same as comparing what the fit uses.

--- GOTCHA: the fitresult must contain saved histograms ---------------------
rabbit_plot_hists.py takes a rabbit FITRESULT (not a datacard), and it needs
histograms saved in it. Our production fits do NOT have them, because
--saveHists and friends were deliberately stripped to avoid the hessian-step
OOM. Symptom: nothing to plot.

Luca's reference DOES have them -- verified directly:
  /ceph/.../lavezzo/alphaS/260717_2D_wallmargin_0/cov/fitresults.hdf5

  hist_prefit_inclusive_variations (and the postfit/global_impacts ones).

4. Then: clip_tensor(res, theory_weight_truncate), res * nominal_weight → filled into ptll_pdfMSHT20mbrange / …mcrange, binned ptll×yll with a pdfVar axis.
5. Reduction, setupRabbit_5TeV.py:975-999: only the extreme members, per the 13 TeV BC_QUARK_MASS_VARIATIONS convention — mbrange pdf1/pdf6, mcrange pdf1/pdf8 (member 0 = set central).
6. Symmetrisation: symmetrize="quadratic", as for PDF uncertainties → each pair becomes SymAvg + SymDiff. 16 raw members → 4 nuisances, group bcQuarkMass (⊂ theory).
7. No 90%→68% CL conversion — deliberate: the MSHT20 range sets are not 90% CL eigenvectors.
8. Application: multiplicative reweight of the MiNNLO weight on top of the CT18Z-corrected nominal — not through SCETlib+DYTurbo (unlike pdfAlphaS and pdfCT18Z, which are).
9. What's plotted: the 4 symmetrised nuisances the fit actually uses, not the 16 raw members.
10. Result: m_c dominates (~6×) and is larger at 5 TeV — 0.935% vs 0.795%; m_b is genuinely smaller — 0.143% vs 0.267%. So "smaller at 5 TeV" holds for m_b only.
